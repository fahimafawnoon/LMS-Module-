## SECTION 1: COURSE IDENTITY

### Course Title
**"The Swivel Way: Premium Mindset & Client-Ready Communication"**

### Course Tagline
Every client-facing interaction you have is a direct expression of the Swivel brand — this course gives you the standards, language, and mindset to make every one count.

### Course Overview
At Swivel, every conversation is a premium experience — or it's a missed opportunity. This course introduces you to the mindset, language standards, and communication behaviors that define what it means to work here. You'll learn how to carry the Swivel brand in every client interaction, handle high-stakes conversations with confidence, and represent our values before you've sent a single email or joined your first call. By the end, you'll know exactly what "client-ready" looks like here and have the tools to get there on day one.

### MODULE 2: The Three Pillars of Swivel's Premium Mindset
```
Duration:     10 minutes
Content Type: Video + Slide Deck (learner-paced)
Format:       Narrated slides with embedded 2-minute scenario video
Purpose:      Introduce and define the three mindset pillars; give learners a mental model to anchor all future content.

THE THREE PILLARS (core content framework):
  Pillar 1 — OWNERSHIP: I am the client's experience, not just part of it.
  Pillar 2 — PRECISION: Vague is never premium. Every word, timeline, and commitment is exact.
  Pillar 3 — WARMTH WITH AUTHORITY: Approachable doesn't mean uncertain. We are confident and kind.

CONTENT OUTLINE:
  1. Introduce the three pillars with visual framework (2 min)
  2. Pillar 1: Ownership — what it looks like, what it sounds like, common failure modes (2.5 min)
  3. Pillar 2: Precision — language standards, why ambiguity costs trust (2.5 min)
  4. Pillar 3: Warmth with Authority — tone calibration, the "confident + kind" model (2.5 min)
  5. The pillars in context: quick 90-second scenario showing all three in a single interaction (1.5 min)

LEARNING ACTIVITY:
  Type:     Knowledge check (2 questions, ungraded, immediate feedback)
  Prompt 1: "A client emails asking for a timeline update. Which response best demonstrates Precision?"
             [4 response options — see Assessment section for full question]
  Prompt 2: "Which of the following describes the Ownership pillar in practice?"
             [4 options]
  Purpose:  Confirm comprehension before application modules; flag conceptual gaps early.

COMPLETION TRIGGER:
  All slides advanced + knowledge check submitted (any score — this is formative)
```

### MODULE 3: What Client-Ready Looks Like (Model the Behavior)
```
Duration:     10 minutes
Content Type: Scenario Video + Guided Observation
Format:       Split-screen "contrast" video showing off-brand vs. on-brand interactions, followed by debrief slides
Purpose:      Show the standards in action so learners can recognize and name the behaviors before they practice them.

CONTENT OUTLINE:
  1. Setup: "You're going to watch two versions of the same interaction" — 30 sec framing
  2. Version A — Off-brand interaction (email exchange + phone call): vague, reactive, slightly dismissive tone (3 min)
  3. Pause + observation prompt: "What specifically felt off? Name it before we continue." (30 sec)
  4. Version B — On-brand interaction: same scenario, Swivel standards applied (3 min)
  5. Debrief: annotated comparison — exactly what changed and why it matters (2 min)
  6. Key language examples: 6 specific phrases/words that signal each pillar in real conversation (1 min)

LEARNING ACTIVITY:
  Type:     Observation worksheet (downloadable PDF or in-LMS form)
  Prompt:   "List two specific things you noticed in Version B that were absent in Version A. For each one, name which Swivel pillar it demonstrates."
  Purpose:  Moves learner from passive watching to active analysis; creates a reference artifact.

COMPLETION TRIGGER:
  Video 100% watched + observation activity submitted
```

### MODULE 4: Your Turn — Apply the Standards
```
Duration:     12 minutes
Content Type: Branching Scenario (interactive)
Format:       Text + audio scenario with 3 decision points; each path has a consequence + coaching note
Purpose:      Give learners a low-stakes environment to apply the three pillars and experience the consequences of off-brand choices.

SCENARIO SETUP:
  "You are three weeks into your role. A key client, Marcus, emails at 4:45pm on a Friday.
   He's frustrated — a deliverable he expected this week isn't in his inbox.
   You have three decisions to make in the next 24 hours. Each one will either build or erode trust."

DECISION POINTS:
  Decision 1: How do you respond to Marcus's Friday email?
    Option A: Reply Monday morning with a full explanation [→ Consequence: client escalates over weekend]
    Option B: Reply within the hour with a brief acknowledgment + commitment to specific next step [→ Correct path]
    Option C: Forward to your manager without replying [→ Consequence: Marcus feels ignored]

  Decision 2: Your manager asks what happened with the deliverable. How do you explain it?
    Option A: "The timeline slipped — I'll handle it." [→ Vague; misses Precision pillar]
    Option B: "The review took longer than scoped. I've already emailed Marcus and committed to Tuesday EOD." [→ Correct: Ownership + Precision]
    Option C: "I'm still figuring out the details." [→ Consequence: manager loses confidence]

  Decision 3: You send Marcus the deliverable Tuesday at 3pm. What do you say?
    Option A: "Here's the file." [→ Misses warmth, no context]
    Option B: "Hi Marcus — attached is the [deliverable name], completed as committed. I've included a brief summary of what's inside. Let me know if you have questions." [→ Correct: all three pillars]
    Option C: "Sorry for the delay — here it is, finally." [→ Undermines confidence, unnecessary self-deprecation]

COACHING NOTES:
  Each incorrect path surfaces a 2–3 sentence coaching note explaining the impact and redirecting.
  Learners can explore all paths before completing — exploration encouraged.

COMPLETION TRIGGER:
  All three decision points reached + scenario completed (any path)
```

## SECTION 7: ASSESSMENT QUESTIONS — SWV-001 BANK

> Full pool: 15 questions. 10 served per attempt, randomized.
> Questions below are the first 10 of the pool.

---

**Q1.** Which of the following best describes the Ownership pillar of the Swivel Premium Mindset?

- A) Ensuring you complete your assigned tasks before the deadline
- B) Treating every client interaction as your personal responsibility, regardless of who else is involved
- C) Taking credit for positive client outcomes and escalating negative ones to your manager
- D) Owning your mistakes by apologizing when something goes wrong

**Correct Answer: B**
**Rationale:** Ownership means the client's experience is yours to shape — not just the tasks assigned to you. Options A and D are partial definitions; C describes the opposite of ownership.
**Objective:** #1 | **Bloom's Level:** Understand

---

**Q2.** A client asks for a project timeline update. Which response best demonstrates the Precision pillar?

- A) "We're on track and should have something to you soon."
- B) "Things are moving along well — I'll be in touch when there's news."
- C) "The final report will be in your inbox by Thursday, November 14th at 3pm ET. If anything changes, I'll let you know by EOD Wednesday."
- D) "I'm still confirming the details but it's looking good."

**Correct Answer: C**
**Rationale:** Precision means specific dates, times, and clear escalation paths — not reassuring language. Options A, B, and D use vague language that leaves the client without actionable information.
**Objective:** #2 | **Bloom's Level:** Apply

---

**Q3.** You receive a tense email from a client who feels their concern wasn't addressed in the last meeting. You know the issue was actually resolved — there was a miscommunication. What is the most Swivel-aligned first response?

- A) Reply with the documentation proving the issue was resolved.
- B) Forward to your manager to handle since it involves a misunderstanding.
- C) Acknowledge the client's concern directly, confirm you'll clarify the resolution, and commit to a specific follow-up time.
- D) Call the client immediately and explain what actually happened.

**Correct Answer: C**
**Rationale:** The first move is acknowledgment + commitment — not proof or escalation. Option D may be appropriate as a follow-up, but launching immediately into an explanation can feel defensive. The client needs to feel heard before they'll hear your resolution.
**Objective:** #2, #3 | **Bloom's Level:** Apply

---

**Q4.** Which of the following interactions demonstrates Warmth with Authority?

- A) "I completely understand your frustration. I'll do my absolute best to get this sorted out for you."
- B) "I hear you. Here's what we're going to do: I'll have a revised proposal to you by Friday at noon. Does that work for you?"
- C) "This type of issue is outside my normal scope, but I'll see what I can do."
- D) "No problem at all! I'll figure something out."

**Correct Answer: B**
**Rationale:** Warmth with Authority combines acknowledgment with a specific, confident commitment. Option A is warm but lacks authority (vague commitment, apologetic tone). Options C and D lack confidence and precision.
**Objective:** #1, #2 | **Bloom's Level:** Apply

---

**Q5.** During a client call, you notice the client sounds frustrated but hasn't explicitly said so. Which response reflects the highest level of client-readiness?

- A) Continue with your agenda — don't presume to know how the client is feeling.
- B) Ask directly: "It sounds like there might be some concerns. Can we take a moment to address those first?"
- C) Wrap the call quickly and follow up by email to avoid an uncomfortable conversation.
- D) Mention the frustration to your manager after the call so they can follow up.

**Correct Answer: B**
**Rationale:** Premium client communication means addressing what's in the room, not just what's on the agenda. Naming the dynamic directly (B) demonstrates both ownership and warmth with authority. Options A, C, and D are forms of avoidance.
**Objective:** #3 | **Bloom's Level:** Analyze

---

**Q6.** Which of the following communication behaviors would be considered off-brand at Swivel?

- A) Sending a brief acknowledgment email within the hour when you can't fully respond yet
- B) Saying "I'm not sure, but I'll find out by tomorrow and come back to you"
- C) Copying your manager on an email to a client without telling the client why
- D) Summarizing next steps at the end of a client meeting in writing

**Correct Answer: C**
**Rationale:** Adding undisclosed CC's creates a perception of surveillance and erodes trust. All other options demonstrate transparency, precision, and responsive ownership.
**Objective:** #2 | **Bloom's Level:** Analyze

---

**Q7.** A new colleague asks you what "client-ready" means at Swivel. Which explanation is most accurate?

- A) Being available to respond to clients at any hour and prioritizing speed above all else
- B) Presenting professionally, keeping email responses short, and avoiding mistakes
- C) Embodying ownership, precision, and warmth with authority in every interaction so clients consistently feel well-served and confident in Swivel
- D) Knowing the client's account history and being prepared to discuss it on any call

**Correct Answer: C**
**Rationale:** "Client-ready" is a whole-person standard built on the three pillars — it's not limited to availability (A), surface presentation (B), or knowledge (D), though those may be components.
**Objective:** #1 | **Bloom's Level:** Understand

---

**Q8.** You've just joined a client call and realize the client is unhappy with work produced before you joined the team. You had no involvement in the issue. What is the most Swivel-aligned response?

- A) Clarify that you weren't involved and explain who was responsible.
- B) Listen fully, acknowledge the client's experience, and take ownership of getting to resolution — regardless of your prior involvement.
- C) Promise to investigate and report back to your manager before making any commitments.
- D) Agree that the previous work was substandard so the client feels validated.

**Correct Answer: B**
**Rationale:** Ownership doesn't require prior involvement — it requires stepping into the client's experience and owning resolution forward. Option A deflects responsibility; C avoids commitment; D inappropriately validates criticism that may not be warranted.
**Objective:** #1, #3 | **Bloom's Level:** Evaluate

---

**Q9.** Which of the following email subject lines best demonstrates the Precision pillar?

- A) "Following up on our conversation"
- B) "Quick update"
- C) "Project Phoenix — Revised Timeline & Next Steps for Review by Nov 14"
- D) "Checking in!"

**Correct Answer: C**
**Rationale:** A precision-standard subject line tells the reader exactly what's inside, what action is required, and by when — before they even open the email. Options A, B, and D are vague and increase cognitive load for the client.
**Objective:** #2 | **Bloom's Level:** Apply

---

**Q10.** Scenario: A client's deliverable was delayed because of an internal tool failure — not human error. When communicating this to the client, what is the Swivel-aligned approach?

- A) Explain the tool failure in detail so the client understands it wasn't your fault.
- B) Apologize without providing context to keep the message simple.
- C) Acknowledge the delay, provide a brief honest explanation, state the new delivery date, and explain what you've done to prevent recurrence.
- D) Wait until you have the deliverable ready before saying anything — no need to raise a concern that's already being resolved.

**Correct Answer: C**
**Rationale:** Premium communication means proactive transparency: acknowledge, explain briefly, commit specifically, and demonstrate forward action. Option A is defensive; B lacks information clients need to plan; D delays communication the client is entitled to.
**Objective:** #2, #3 | **Bloom's Level:** Evaluate
