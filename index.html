Section 1 Course Overview ▼

At Swivel, every conversation is a premium experience — or it's a missed opportunity. This course introduces you to the mindset, language standards, and communication behaviors that define what it means to work here.

You'll learn how to carry the Swivel brand in every client interaction, handle high-stakes conversations with confidence, and represent our values before you've sent a single email or joined your first call.

By the end, you'll know exactly what "client-ready" looks like here — and you'll have the tools to get there on day one.

The Three Pillars

Ownership
I am the client's experience, not just part of it.
Precision
Vague is never premium. Every word, timeline, and commitment is exact.
Warmth with Authority
Approachable doesn't mean uncertain. We are confident and kind.
🎯

Section 3 Module Breakdown — 52 Minutes Total ▼
1
Welcome to the Swivel Standard
6 min Video
Set the stakes. Establish relevance. Answer "why does this matter?" before anything else.
  • Direct message from senior leadership — why communication standards exist at Swivel (2 min)
  • What "premium" actually means here — not luxury, but intentionality (2 min)
  • Course roadmap and the question to hold throughout: "Would a Swivel client feel well-served by this?" (2 min)
Reflection Prompt: "Think of one interaction you've had as a client that felt genuinely premium. What made it feel that way?"
Completion: Video 100% watched (no skip)
2
The Three Pillars of the Premium Mindset
10 min Video + Slides
Introduce the primary mental model. Give learners an anchor they'll use in every subsequent module.
  • Visual framework introducing all three pillars (2 min)
  • Pillar 1 — Ownership: what it looks like, sounds like, and where it fails (2.5 min)
  • Pillar 2 — Precision: language standards, why ambiguity costs trust (2.5 min)
  • Pillar 3 — Warmth with Authority: tone calibration, the "confident + kind" model (2.5 min)
  • 90-second scenario demonstrating all three pillars in a single client interaction (1.5 min)
Knowledge Check (×2, ungraded): Which response demonstrates Precision? / Which describes Ownership in practice?
Completion: All slides advanced + knowledge check submitted
3
What Client-Ready Looks Like (Model the Behavior)
10 min Scenario Video
Show standards in action so learners can recognize and name behaviors before they practice them.
  • Setup: "You'll watch two versions of the same client interaction" (30 sec)
  • Version A — Off-brand: vague, reactive, mildly dismissive tone (3 min)
  • Pause: "What specifically felt off? Name it." (30 sec)
  • Version B — On-brand: same scenario, Swivel standards applied (3 min)
  • Annotated debrief: exactly what changed and why it matters (2 min)
  • 6 specific phrases that signal each pillar in real conversation (1 min)
Observation Worksheet: "List two specific things present in Version B that were absent in Version A. Name the Swivel pillar each demonstrates."
Completion: Video 100% watched + observation activity submitted
4
Your Turn — Apply the Standards
12 min Branching Scenario
Low-stakes practice environment. Apply the three pillars. Experience consequences of off-brand choices.

Scenario: You're 3 weeks in. Key client Marcus emails at 4:45pm Friday — his deliverable wasn't delivered. You have 3 decisions over the next 24 hours.

  • Decision 1: How do you respond to Marcus's Friday email? (Ownership test)
  • Decision 2: How do you explain the situation to your manager? (Precision test)
  • Decision 3: How do you deliver the Tuesday follow-up? (All three pillars)
Branching Scenario: 3 decision points, each with 3 options and coaching feedback on incorrect paths. All paths explorable.
Completion: All three decision points reached + scenario completed
5
Making It Yours — Personal Application
8 min Reading + Reflection
Bridge course content to the learner's actual role, relationships, and next real interaction.
  • "The Gap Between Knowing and Doing" — why mindset training fails without deliberate practice (2 min read)
  • The 30-Day Communication Audit: three behaviors to track in your own work (2 min read)
  • Action Plan completion — "This isn't homework. This is your performance plan." (3.5 min)
Action Plan Submission (see Section 6): Written commitments stored in learner record. Optional share with manager.
Completion: Time-on-page ≥ 3 min + Action Plan submitted
6
Assessment + Certification
6 min Graded Assessment
Verify learning. Produce a completion record. Close on a forward-facing note.
  • 10 questions drawn from a randomized pool of 15
  • Mix of multiple choice + scenario-based ("select best response") questions
  • Immediate per-question feedback with rationale
  • Up to 3 attempts — failure path routes to specific review modules
  • Auto-generated certificate on pass: "Swivel Communication Standards Certified"
Closing Message: "You now have the language, mindset, and standards that define the Swivel experience. Every client interaction from here is a chance to make those real. Go be client-ready."
Completion: Assessment passed at ≥ 80%
✅

Section 7 Sample Assessment Questions (5 of 15) ▼

Full question bank contains 15 questions. 10 served per attempt, randomized. All questions test Apply, Analyze, or Evaluate — not recall. Correct answers shown for design review purposes.

Q1 Which of the following best describes the Ownership pillar of the Swivel Premium Mindset?
AEnsuring you complete your assigned tasks before the deadline
BTreating every client interaction as your personal responsibility, regardless of who else is involved
CTaking credit for positive outcomes and escalating negative ones to your manager
DOwning your mistakes by apologizing when something goes wrong
Why B: Ownership means the client's experience is yours to shape — not just the tasks assigned to you. Options A and D are partial definitions; C describes the opposite of ownership.
Objective 1 Understand
Q2 A client asks for a project timeline update. Which response best demonstrates the Precision pillar?
A"We're on track and should have something to you soon."
B"Things are moving along well — I'll be in touch when there's news."
C"The final report will be in your inbox by Thursday, November 14th at 3pm ET. If anything changes, I'll let you know by EOD Wednesday."
D"I'm still confirming the details but it's looking good."
Why C: Precision means specific dates, times, and clear escalation paths — not reassuring language. Options A, B, and D use vague language that leaves the client without actionable information.
Objective 2 Apply
Q3 You receive a tense email from a client who feels their concern wasn't addressed. You know the issue was resolved — there was a miscommunication. What is the most Swivel-aligned first response?
AReply with the documentation proving the issue was resolved
BForward to your manager to handle since it involves a misunderstanding
CAcknowledge the client's concern directly, confirm you'll clarify the resolution, and commit to a specific follow-up time
DCall the client immediately and explain what actually happened
Why C: The first move is acknowledgment + commitment — not proof or escalation. Option D may be appropriate as a follow-up, but launching immediately into an explanation can feel defensive. The client needs to feel heard before they'll hear your resolution.
Objectives 2, 3 Apply
Q4 Which of the following interactions demonstrates Warmth with Authority?
A"I completely understand your frustration. I'll do my absolute best to get this sorted out for you."
B"I hear you. Here's what we're going to do: I'll have a revised proposal to you by Friday at noon. Does that work for you?"
C"This type of issue is outside my normal scope, but I'll see what I can do."
D"No problem at all! I'll figure something out."
Why B: Warmth with Authority combines acknowledgment with a specific, confident commitment. Option A is warm but lacks authority. Options C and D lack confidence and precision.
Objectives 1, 2 Apply
Q5 During a client call, you notice the client sounds frustrated but hasn't explicitly said so. Which response reflects the highest level of client-readiness?
AContinue with your agenda — don't presume to know how the client is feeling
BAsk directly: "It sounds like there might be some concerns. Can we take a moment to address those first?"
CWrap the call quickly and follow up by email to avoid an uncomfortable conversation
DMention the frustration to your manager after the call so they can follow up
Why B: Premium client communication means addressing what's in the room, not just what's on the agenda. Naming the dynamic directly demonstrates both Ownership and Warmth with Authority. Options A, C, and D are forms of avoidance.
Objective 3 Analyze

Reusable LMS Module Template

Duplicate this structure for every new course. Replace highlighted placeholders. Keep all sections intact.

01

Course Identity

Title Formula

[Action Verb Phrase]: [Specific Skill or Mindset] for [Target Audience]
  • Lead with a verb or outcome-signal word (e.g., "Mastering," "Building," "The [Brand] Way")
  • Keep it under 10 words
  • Avoid: "leveraging," "synergizing," "next-level"

Overview Formula (75–100 words)

[PAIN POINT / CONTEXT]. This course gives you [specific tools/frameworks] to [measurable outcome]. By the end, you will [capability #1] and [capability #2] — skills that apply [immediately / on your next interaction].
02

Learning Objectives

  • Write exactly 3–4 objectives
  • Lead each with a Bloom's action verb — never "understand" or "know"
  • Each objective must be independently verifiable in assessment
  • Map each objective to at least one module and one quiz question
[Bloom's Verb] + [specific skill/behavior] + [context/condition] Examples: Apply Swivel's communication standards to written scenarios… Identify the critical moments in a client interaction where tone determines satisfaction… Construct a personal 30-day action plan aligned to [Company] standards…
✓ Strong Verbs
  • Apply, Demonstrate, Execute
  • Analyze, Distinguish, Compare
  • Evaluate, Justify, Prioritize
  • Construct, Design, Develop
✗ Avoid
  • Understand (untestable)
  • Know (not observable)
  • Be aware of (passive)
  • Appreciate (subjective)

Tech Specs · Action Plan · Assessment · Metadata

Quick Checklist — Before Development Handoff

  • 3–4 learning objectives — all Bloom's Apply level or above
  • All 6 module durations specified (not ranges)
  • Completion triggers defined for every module
  • Assessment question pool: 1.5× final count (15 questions for 10-question test)
  • Every question tests application or analysis — zero recall-only questions
  • Action Plan includes free-text commitments + specific accountability dates
  • Metadata short description ≤ 160 characters
  • Annual review date set in metadata

Common Mistakes

MistakeFix
Objectives using "understand"Replace with Apply / Analyze / Evaluate verb
Module durations as rangesPick one target number
Completion criteria that can't be enforcedTie to a measurable LMS trigger
Quiz questions testing recallFrame as scenario: "A client does X — what do you do?"
Action plan = rating scales onlyInclude free-text commitments and accountability partners
Module 6 as passive reviewMake it synthesis + forward-facing close
